# MIFF cut-cost precision repair

Observed before changing code: on a two-edge chain costing 5e-13 then 1e-13, the old motor cut the first edge and reported a minimum-cost firewall. At ordinary scale it correctly cut the cheaper second edge. A single edge costing 1e16 produced no cut and `FIREWALL_INCOMPLETE`. The absolute residual threshold (1e-12) and the floating-point super-terminal capacity `total + 1` caused these two failures. Full old/new executions are in `restoration/20260915-miff/observed-comparison.json`.

We downloaded and inspected NetworkX's actual Edmonds-Karp residual update, residual-network construction, and directed-graph expected-value tests. Source URLs, bytes and SHA-256 hashes are in `SOURCES.json`; its BSD license is preserved. The implementation tests residual capacity directly and requires artificial terminal capacities that cannot be a cheaper cut. This inspection supplied algorithmic references; NetworkX itself was not installed or run.

The repaired MIFF keeps ordinary Edmonds-Karp but uses sparse integer residuals. Each supplied binary float is represented by its exact integer ratio; the common denominator is a power of two. Integral costs stay integers. Consequently residual comparisons have no absolute epsilon, and the terminal bound is strictly greater than the sum of real capacities without overflow. Parallel and reverse edges retain their individual identities in the returned cut. The reported total remains a floating-point number; an optimum whose total overflows that output range raises an explicit error.

Independent validation enumerates every admissible vertex partition for 20 small graphs at three power-of-two scales, including parallel/reverse edges and multiple sources/targets. This oracle does not repeat the augmenting-path algorithm. A separate test uses NetworkX's published directed graph with expected cut 23, again at three scales. These are correctness checks, not speed superiority, novelty or real auditor benefit.

MIFF still requires a declared graph, gains and intervention costs. It does not discover document provenance, identify a suspect automatically, enforce cuts in a real application, or compute a Bayesian cut posterior. The contamination diagnostic remains a nonnegative linear feedback model, with its existing stability rule. Every declared edge is treated as an information path even if its numerical gain is zero. Its displayed path list is capped at 100; all-path enumeration can still be expensive in dense graphs. This repair does not claim to solve those remaining modeling and scalability limits.

References: [actual NetworkX Edmonds-Karp implementation](https://github.com/networkx/networkx/blob/main/networkx/algorithms/flow/edmondskarp.py), [residual network code](https://github.com/networkx/networkx/blob/main/networkx/algorithms/flow/utils.py), [independently specified graph tests](https://github.com/networkx/networkx/blob/main/networkx/algorithms/flow/tests/test_maxflow.py).
